The current table routes every report through causeOf. That function groups reports by the tenant’s locale, form version, and channel. It does not inspect the report body. The table therefore places the temporal anomalies under parser and footnotes them there.

The review separates those anomalies from the supported parser group. This is an evidence boundary, not a new root-cause claim.

The three tickets

The shared parser reads day-first dates as month-first

This group covers the reports whose dates swap the day and month. It spans app, email, and form channels. The mechanism matches causeOf: a day-first tenant reaches the shared month-first parser.

Booking v3 gives a month-first hint to day-first tenants

This group covers form reports from day-first tenants that use booking-v3. The form tells people to enter MM/DD/YYYY, then the product reads the date in the tenant’s day-first locale. The hint and the parser disagree.

Temporal anomalies need their own investigation

The desk marked five report IDs for special reading. Four describe an apparent one-day or time-zone symptom. R-41236 says an invite arrived before the booking existed. That symptom does not establish the same failure mechanism.

Keep these reports together only as an investigation queue. Split the ticket again if runtime evidence shows more than one cause.
